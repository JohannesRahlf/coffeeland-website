Following a one-week data analysis training program for PhD students affiliated with the CoffeeLand Project, held from 14–20 September 2026, Dr. Diress Tsegaye Alemu joined the Jimma CoffeeLand Project team for a field visit to coffee-growing areas in the Agaro region of southwestern Ethiopia.

The field visit provided an opportunity to observe the region's diverse coffee production landscapes and gain firsthand insights into coffee cultivation practices and production systems. The visit also highlighted the importance of understanding the different coffee-growing environments in supporting research, sustainable coffee production, and the long-term development of the coffee sector.

Ethiopia, the birthplace of Arabica coffee, is characterized by four major coffee production systems: garden coffee, plantation coffee, semi-forest coffee, and natural forest coffee. These systems represent the country's rich coffee-growing heritage, ecological diversity, and unique production practices.

The photographs capture glimpses of the diverse coffee landscapes explored during the field visit, highlighting the richness and diversity of coffee production in the Agaro area.

The visit forms part of the CoffeeLand Project's broader efforts to strengthen research capacity, promote knowledge exchange, and contribute to the sustainable development of Ethiopia's coffee sector.
